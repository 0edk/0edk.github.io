13 principles of doubt

Many Jews believe something close to Maimonides' 13 Principles of Faith. I do not. Here are the most equivalent statements that I do believe.

  1. There is an entity that created the world, somehow, at some level. It does not share the other attributes associated with "God".
  2. The creator of the universe is separate from the source of prophecy is separate from the author of Torah. These and other aspects of "God" have been erroneously blurred together.
  3. The author of Torah probably has a physical body, but we do not know it and so far cannot access it.
  4. The three aspects of God discussed above need not be eternal, and probably are not.
  5. It is nonsensical to worship God, because the term "God" is ambiguous. You can worship most of the aspects of God, and it may be wise to do so.
  6. Select people, including those who have been labelled "prophets", can access sudden and anomalous wisdom produced by the brain. This knowledge is not (directly) guided by the creator of the universe, or the author of Torah.
  7. Moses had insights in the manner called prophecy, at or above the level of any people known in the Torah.
  8. Torah comes from an uncertain source within the material world. Could be aliens, could be time-travellers, could be a charismatic council of elders.
  9. If Torah is to guide us, we must act as though its law is eternal and minimally mutable.
  10. If Torah is to guide us, we must act as though all our deeds are subject to its laws.
  11. If Torah is to guide us, we must act as though our observance of its law, or failure theretowards, matters.
  12. Repeated and coordinated application of Torah leads to a fixed point likely better than the limit of our current secular order, but still not ideal.
  13. The dead will not be resurrected, unless we do it ourselves.
